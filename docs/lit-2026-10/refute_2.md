## Claim 11: physics, stated precisely

Let d = λ / (2 sin θ), so ln d = ln(λ/2) − ln sin θ.

**Isotropic strain.** Every lattice length scales by (1+ε), so every d_hkl → (1+ε) d_hkl. That gives ln d_hkl → ln d_hkl + ln(1+ε), a single constant offset for all reflections.

- The peak-position pattern ("stick pattern") is therefore an exact rigid translation in log-d.
- A cross-correlation C(τ) evaluated over lag τ, taking the max or a shift-tolerant de Gelder-style kernel, is exactly invariant in its peak positions.
- A **zero-lag** correlation (a plain cosine or Pearson on a fixed grid) is **not** invariant. It degrades once ln(1+ε) exceeds the peak width.

Exactness also fails, at second order, for three reasons:

1. **Peak shapes.** Instrumental widths are roughly fixed in 2θ. Since Δln d = −cot θ · Δθ, the width in log-d is cot θ · FWHM_θ/2, which depends on θ. A strained reflection lands at a different θ and so gets a different log-d width. Low-angle peaks are much wider in log-d.
2. **Intensities.** The Lorentz-polarisation factor, the Debye–Waller factor and absorption all depend on θ. They change slightly as peaks move, and the form factors are evaluated at the new sin θ/λ.
3. **Window edges.** Reflections enter or leave the measured 2θ range. In log-d the window is not shifted along with the pattern.

**Anisotropic strain** (the realistic thermal case, per Bond 2021) moves each reflection by a different amount, so log-d is not invariant to it.

**2θ zero offset δ** (2θ_obs = 2θ_true + δ). To first order, Δln d = −(cot θ)·δ/2, with δ in radians.

- This is **not** constant. It diverges at low angle (cot θ → ∞) and becomes small at high angle (cot 75° ≈ 0.27).
- So a zero offset, which is a uniform shift in 2θ space, becomes a **non-uniform, θ-dependent shift in log-d**. Log-d is not invariant to it.
- The duality is exact: 2θ space makes zero offset uniform and strain non-uniform (Δ2θ ≈ −2ε tan θ). log-d makes strain uniform and zero offset non-uniform. No single 1-D axis makes both uniform.
- Sample displacement (Δ2θ ≈ −2s cos θ / R) is non-uniform in both spaces.
- Practical implication: a log-d model still needs zero-offset augmentation, or an explicit zero-offset parameter in any re-rank. A low-angle-heavy organic pattern is the worst case, since molecular-crystal peaks cluster at 2θ < 30°.
