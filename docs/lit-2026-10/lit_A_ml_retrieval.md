## 2. The five papers that most threaten "learned retrieval beats library search-match for molecular polymorphs"

1. **SMolNet (arXiv 2411.03308).** This is the one real-data organic polymorph benchmark, and the learned model does not beat classical similarity. On 3,750 experimental pharma XRPD patterns (unseen compounds), the learned Siamese model leads on AUROC (0.98 vs 0.92). The classical De Gelder (2001) weighted cross-correlation leads on accuracy (0.92 vs 0.89) and F1 (0.93 vs 0.80), and the learned model has very large fold variance (F1 ±0.38). A reviewer will cite it as "for organic solid forms, classical similarity is already competitive." xrd-clip has to beat **De Gelder weighted cross-correlation**, not only Pearson. It also mirrors the URTC result that full-profile correlation wins on clean data.
2. **MatDiffract (arXiv 2607.20880).** It beats JADE by about 20 pts top-1 (89.0 vs 68.7%) on 875 real patterns using **hand-crafted** peak features plus perturbation augmentation, with no learned encoder. So the "embedding beats SM" story is not evidence for *learning*: a non-learned, shift-tolerant vector index gets there too. xrd-clip needs a hand-crafted-feature retrieval baseline (peak-feature vectors, shift-corrected correlation) and needs to show the learned embedding adds something beyond robustness tricks.
3. **XRDiff (arXiv 2606.14003).** On 148 RRUFF experimental patterns, **full-spectrum** conditioning gets below 1% top-1 while **peak-based** conditioning gets 12.8–15.5%. xrd-clip's 1D ResNet runs on full simulated profiles, which is exactly the encoding that collapsed on real data here. Without an experimental test, a reviewer can argue that xrd-clip's simulated-only robustness may not transfer. (RealPXRD-Solver's d-spacing–intensity fingerprint encoder points the same way.)
4. **PXtal (arXiv 2610.10653).** Its own InfoNCE-CLIP baseline is beaten by 7 pts Acc@1 (67.26→74.45% on MP-100) by unbalanced-OT losses, and the gain is concentrated where distinct structures have near-identical patterns, which is the polymorph-confusion regime. It weakens any claim that a standard CLIP/InfoNCE design is the right tool for within-family discrimination. It also shows the field moving quickly (posted 2026-10-07, Rosseinsky group, which also works on organics/MOFs).
5. **Glick et al., CSP of pharmaceuticals with NN potentials (arXiv 2507.16218).** For drug polymorphs the realistic competitor is *CSP + PXRD matching*. It identifies and ranks polymorphs of three drugs "from PXRD patterns alone" in a semi-blinded challenge, and it works for forms **not** in any library. A library-retrieval method (xrd-clip or SM) cannot find a new polymorph, so the claim must be scoped to "known-form identification."

Honourable mentions: **Gan Jiang (2610.07862)**, where real opXRD top-1 is only 40.83% even for the best agent, so real-data ceilings are low; and **Wei et al. (2609.26502)**, where template retrieval beats generative CSP. The second *supports* a retrieval framing, but also means a strong nearest-neighbour baseline is expected.

Papers that **support** the xrd-clip claim: CPICANN (80% vs JADE 61% experimental), XQueryer (beats SM, 1,003 experimental), GALAXI (beats SM, robust to displacement), RADAR-PD (mismatch tolerance beats Dara), and the withdrawn Xtalyst (peak drift breaks correlation retrieval). **All are inorganic.**

## 3. Techniques worth borrowing

- **Peak/d-spacing-based encodings for sim-to-real transfer** (XRDiff peak descriptors; RealPXRD-Solver d-spacing–intensity fingerprints; RADAR-PD coarse momentum-transfer fingerprints). Add a peak-set or Q-space encoder branch, or a hybrid, and test it on real organic patterns.
- **Unbalanced-OT / generalized-KL contrastive loss** (PXtal) instead of InfoNCE. It targets the "distinct structures, similar patterns" asymmetry that drives within-polymorph confusion. Cheap to try as a loss swap.
- **SigLIP-style sigmoid pairwise loss** (best in SMolNet on real pharma XRPD). Also simple to swap in, and it handles many near-duplicate negatives (same-family polymorphs) more gracefully **[inferred]**.
- **Physics-informed augmentation**: strain, texture/preferred orientation, crystallite size, sample displacement, impurity fractions (XRD-AutoAnalyzer, GALAXI, MatDiffract perturbations, Schuetzke IUCrJ 2021). Also on-the-fly synthetic-crystal generation (Schopmans et al.).
- **Retrieve-then-verify**: shortlist with embeddings, then re-rank by Rietveld or a shift-corrected profile fit (MatDiffract: 89.0→91.3% top-1; GALAXI; AGAPI-XRD; Gan Jiang). This turns xrd-clip's weakness (correlation wins on clean data) into a two-stage design: embedding for robust shortlist, correlation/Pawley for final rank.
- **Calibrated / probabilistic outputs and multiple hypotheses** (Maffettone ensemble; Dara multi-hypothesis). Report calibrated confidence on within-family retrieval.
- **Baselines a reviewer will expect**: De Gelder weighted cross-correlation; JADE/HighScore-style SM or the open XMatcher (with shift correction) or Dara; a hand-crafted peak-feature vector index (MatDiffract-style); plain InfoNCE vs OT loss.
- **Evaluation protocols**: XRDiff's "all polymorphs of a composition held out together" split; SMolNet's leave-compounds-out split; opXRD and RRUFF as real sets (organic coverage of opXRD not confirmed).

## 4. Gaps that appear open (with searches)

**G1. Learned PXRD↔structure retrieval (contrastive/CLIP) for organic molecular crystals from the CSD, evaluated against search-match.** Nothing found. The CSD-trained PXRD models found are generative/regressive (UstcUnfold 2411.06062) or symmetry classifiers (XRDecoupler 2511.06976). Every contrastive PXRD↔structure model found (XtalNet, XCCP, PXRDGen encoder, MatBind, UniMat, PXtal) is inorganic or MOF.
**G2. Within-polymorph-family retrieval as an explicit metric.** No paper reports retrieval restricted to same-compound polymorph sets. Closest: the XRDiff polymorph-held-out split (inorganic, generative), PXtal's "nonpaired neighbour" analysis (inorganic), and SMolNet pairwise same/different-form classification (organic, but classification and not structure retrieval).
**G3. Controlled robustness curves (2θ zero-offset, lattice strain) comparing a learned embedding vs Pearson / peak-list SM.** No paper found with these sweeps for organics. For inorganics: MatDiffract and XMatcher correct shift classically, GALAXI reports displacement robustness, and the withdrawn Xtalyst reports that drift correction doubles correlation.
